# Lesson 12: Conditionals and where to go next

One last piece of grammar: two useful ways to say **if**.

## Grammar

### Daefer: if this is the case...

Use **daefer** for a real possibility or cause-and-effect condition.

`daefer run i anvu, i anvu su`

<details class="example-translation">
<summary>Click to Reveal Translation</summary>

*If you go, I go too.*

</details>

### Iliciu: imagine this instead...

**iliciu** means *imagine*. It sets up a hypothetical or counterfactual situation.

`iliciu a nim a run, i anvu`

<details class="example-translation">
<summary>Click to Reveal Translation</summary>

*If I were you, I would go.*

</details>

Notice that by using **daefer** or **iliciu**, we avoid many complicated verb forms in other languages such as English.

## Vocabulary

### Building blocks

**ELI** — virtue. *Mnemonic: **E**thical **L**iving **I**deals.*  
**VER** — safety, security. *Mnemonic: French **verrou**, a lock or bolt.*  
**ELO** — social virtues. *Mnemonic: Portuguese **elo**, a link or bond.*  

### Words

| Oravia | Meaning | Breakdown |
|---|---|---|
| **elireva** | truth, true | **ELI** virtue + **REVA** accurate/real |
| **anever** | secure, safe | **ANE** static action + **VER** safety |
| **eloele** | freedom, free | **ELO** social virtues + **ELE** self-expansion |
| **iliciu** | imagine | **ILI** cognition + **CIU** creative product |

## Put it together

!!! tip "From earlier lessons"
    **beivu** = car  
    **do / ando** = can  
    **ciureva** = photo  
    **anidai** = want  
    **eleyel** = love  
    **yudur** = difficult

`daefer a beivu a anever, i do i anvu`

<details class="example-translation">
<summary>Click to Reveal Translation</summary>

*If the car is safe, I can go.*

</details>

`iliciu a nim a run, i anro, eta i anvu`

<details class="example-translation">
<summary>Click to Reveal Translation</summary>

*If I were you, I would decide and then go.*

</details>

`i ilian ca a ciureva a elireva`

<details class="example-translation">
<summary>Click to Reveal Translation</summary>

*I know that the picture is accurate / true to reality.*

</details>

`i eleyel e eloele`

<details class="example-translation">
<summary>Click to Reveal Translation</summary>

*I love freedom.*

</details>

## How much Oravia do you know now?

Oravia is fast to learn, and it was designed with a purposefully small vocabulary that is very powerful combined with the grammatical and expressive features of the language. It has about ~860 dictionary words built mostly from ~260 building blocks. In these twelve lessons you've met about 65 building blocks, roughly a quarter of the building-block system.

The six words in Lesson 11 were never taught as vocabulary. More than 40 other dictionary words also have breakdowns made entirely from pieces in this short course. 

### The simple form is usually enough

This course has mostly used Oravia's simple core.

When you need more precision, you can add it: mark singular or plural explicitly, distinguish moving **into** a place from moving **toward** it, or make other distinctions that these twelve lessons haven't covered. Most everyday sentences work fine with the simple form. The philosophy is a simple system that has options for disambiguation or more precision if needed.

**yalwol** — wide

**yaltan** — big  
**leipili** — flower  
**oipili** — cute  
**ilaluan** — say / tell  
**eleyel** — love

`Lis, nim su eofa i anvu de wiltan en lealei.`  
`A lealei a yalwol, su a leirih a yaltan.`  
`En lealei, a leipili a oipili.`  
`Eofa i ilaluan ca hay i eleyel e lealei.`  
`Nim visomo i anifou e mo, eta nim i apasoi.`  
`Lisoi, nim su eofa i anvu en wiltan.`  
`En wiltan, i anona e leipili u fasu.`  
`Fasu i ilaluan ca a leipili a oipili.`

<details class="example-translation" markdown="1">
<summary>Reveal the reading</summary>

*One day, my friend and I go from the city to the forest.*  
*The forest is wide, and the tree is big.*  
*In the forest, a flower is cute.*  
*My friend says that they love the forest.*  
*My stomach needs food, so I hurry.*  
*Soon, my friend and I go to the city.*  
*In the city, I give the flower to my sibling.*  
*My sibling says that the flower is cute.*

</details>
