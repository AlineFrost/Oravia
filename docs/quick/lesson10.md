# Lesson 10: AR & IS

Oravia usually tells you *when* something happens with a time expression: yesterday, next week, or a general word such as **lar** and **lis**. The verb itself can stay plain.

## Grammar

### Start with the time expression

A few useful time expressions are:

**litamar** — yesterday  
**litamis** — tomorrow  
**lar** — once / at some unspecified time in the past  
**lis** — one day / at some unspecified time in the future

`litamar, i mo e moalen`

<details class="example-translation">
<summary>Click to Reveal Translation</summary>

*Yesterday, I ate a banana.*

</details>

`lis, i mo e moalen`

<details class="example-translation">
<summary>Click to Reveal Translation</summary>

*One day, I will eat a banana.*

</details>

The time word carries the past or future reference, so that **mo** itself stays bare.

### -ar and -is

Use **-ar** when an action is already complete by the time you are talking about:

`i moar`

<details class="example-translation">
<summary>Click to Reveal Translation</summary>

*I have eaten / I already ate.*

</details>

Use **-is** when an action is still later than that point:

`i mois`

<details class="example-translation">
<summary>Click to Reveal Translation</summary>

*I have not eaten yet / I am going to eat.*

</details>

With the present as the reference, these can look a little like past and future. The distinction becomes clearer when you compare two events.

A quick picture:

`[ action-ar ]  →  reference point  →  [ action-is ]`

**-ar** points to something already on the earlier/completed side.  
**-is** points to something still on the later/not-yet-started side.

### Before and after another event

Start with two events in the same past scene:

`litamar, nim i mo cali haya i anvu`

<details class="example-translation">
<summary>Click to Reveal Translation</summary>

*Yesterday, I was eating when they went.*

</details>

Now add **-ar** to the first event:

`litamar, nim i moar cali haya i anvu`

<details class="example-translation">
<summary>Click to Reveal Translation</summary>

*Yesterday, I had already eaten when they went.*

</details>

The eating is complete by the time of the going.

!!! question "What changed?"
    Compare the two sentences. Everything is the same except **mo → moar**. That one suffix tells you the eating was **already finished** at the second event.

Or put **-is** on the second event:

`litamar, nim i mo, haya i anvuis`

<details class="example-translation">
<summary>Click to Reveal Translation</summary>

*Yesterday, when I ate, they had not gone yet / they went later.*

</details>

Notice that this avoids a lot of complicated verb tenses in languages such as English.

## Vocabulary

### Building blocks

**GEH (subcluster)** — sales. *Mnemonic: **G**oods **E**xchange **H**ands.*  
**YO** — colors. *Mnemonic: **Y**ellow, **O**range.*  
**YAR** — light. *Mnemonic: **RAY** backwards.*  
**LEA** — landscape. *Mnemonic: English **lea**, “meadow.”*  
**REL** — up, high. *Mnemonic: **rise / elevate**.*  

### Words

| Oravia | Meaning | Breakdown |
|---|---|---|
| **gehna** | buy | **GEH** sales + **NA** giving |
| **yoyar** | white | **YO** colors + **YAR** light |
| **leayo** | garden | **LEA** landscape + **YO** colors |
| **iliro** | think | **ILI** cognition + **RO** thinking |
| **borel** | ceiling | **BOR** house structure + **REL** up/high |

### Try liyar

You already knew **LI** = time. Now you know **YAR** = light.

What time of day does **liyar** suggest?

<details>
<summary>Reveal</summary>

**liyar** = **morning, daytime**.

</details>

## Put it together

!!! tip "From earlier lessons"
    **moalen** = banana  
    **anvu** = go  
    **cali** = when  

`litamar, i mo e moalen`

<details class="example-translation">
<summary>Click to Reveal Translation</summary>

*Yesterday, I ate a banana.*

</details>

`a borel a yoyar`

<details class="example-translation">
<summary>Click to Reveal Translation</summary>

*The ceiling is white.*

</details>

`i iliro ca a leayo a yaltan`

<details class="example-translation">
<summary>Click to Reveal Translation</summary>

*I think the garden is big.*

</details>

`litamis, nim i boemo, haya i mois`

<details class="example-translation">
<summary>Click to Reveal Translation</summary>

*Tomorrow, I cook, they will not have eaten yet / they eat later (eating, with -is, happens after the time of the other action.*

</details>

`litamis, nim i boemoar cali haya i mo`

<details class="example-translation">
<summary>Click to Reveal Translation</summary>

*Tomorrow, I will have already cooked when they eat (cooking, with -ar, completed by the time of the other action).*

</details>

`lis, i anvu en leayo`

<details class="example-translation">
<summary>Click to Reveal Translation</summary>

*One day, I will go to the garden.*

</details>
