# Lesson 5: U Marker, De, En

Suppose you say **I give the food**. The obvious next question is: *to whom?*

## Grammar

### U: to / for

That recipient gets **u**:

**u** — indirect object, usually *to* or *for*. Mnemonic: **U = Unto**.

For the example below, **anona** means *give*. Its breakdown comes later in the lesson.

Now the whole mnemonic finally fits:

**Actor Initiates, Engaging, Unto.**

| Marker | Mnemonic | Main role |
|---|---|---|
| **a** | **Actor** | subject |
| **i** | **Initiates** | verb |
| **e** | **Engaging** | direct object |
| **u** | **Unto** | indirect object: to / for |

`a nim i anona e mo u eofa`

<details class="example-translation">
<summary>Click to Reveal Translation</summary>

*I give food to a friend.*

</details>

Because the markers show the roles, Oravia has flexible word order. Other orders are just as acceptable, and speakers can choose the one that feels comfortable or matches the style they want to use. In this short English-language course, most examples follow English-like order to keep it simple. 

### De: of / from

**de** connects something to a source, origin, or possessor:

`boemo de eofa`

<details class="example-translation">
<summary>Click to Reveal Translation</summary>

*the friend's kitchen / the kitchen of the friend*

</details>

Personal pronouns still use the shorter pattern from Lesson 1:

`nim boemo`

<details class="example-translation">
<summary>Click to Reveal Translation</summary>

*my kitchen*

</details>

It may help you remember to think of Romance *de*.

### En: in / on / at

**en** covers ordinary location and time relations such as *in, on,* and *at*:

`nim en boemo`

<details class="example-translation">
<summary>Click to Reveal Translation</summary>

*I am in the kitchen.*

</details>

**bo** means *house / home*. 

`nim en bo`

<details class="example-translation">
<summary>Click to Reveal Translation</summary>

*I am at home.*

</details>

Notice that there is no **a ... a ...** here (is/are), because **en** already expresses the location.

To remember, think about *en* being similar to *in*, or Spanish *en*.

### How much do you actually have to mark?

In ordinary speech, markers may be dropped, as you already saw with **a nim i mo → nim i mo**. Use a marker whenever it helps make the role clear.

## Vocabulary

### Subclusters: a narrower kind of thing

A cluster says *what general kind of thing is this?* A **subcluster** zooms in one step further.

The AN family makes it easy to see:

**AN** — action in general  
**ANE** — static action: staying, resting, remaining  
**ANI** — movement toward  
**ANO** — movement away  

So to keep this in mind: **ANI-** points toward, and **ANO-** points away.

### Building blocks

**ANE** — static action. *Mnemonic: **E = at Ease**, staying in place.*  
**FENE** — rest. *Mnemonic: **FINish**, then rest.*  
**ANO** — movement away. *Mnemonic: **O = Out**.*  
**NA** — giving. *Mnemonic: “**N**ow, **A**ccept this.”*  
**BOR** — structural parts of a house. *Mnemonic: the **border** of the house.*  
**LU** — outside. *Mnemonic: **Look Up** when you are outside.*  

### Words

| Oravia | Meaning | Breakdown |
|---|---|---|
| **anefene** | rest | **ANE** static action + **FENE** rest |
| **anona** | give | **ANO** movement away + **NA** giving |
| **borlu** | window | **BOR** house structure + **LU** outside |
| **leigai** | shrub | **LEI** plants + **GAI** small |
| **vardur** | tooth | **VAR** face/head area + **DUR** hardness |
