# Before You Start

Welcome!   
This course was designed to give you a feel of how Oravia works and what it is about. By the end, you will know all the basics, which is enough to start communicating!

Before Lesson 1, take a few minutes to hear how Oravia sounds and notice one thing about its vocabulary.

## How to use this course

This course is meant to be light. Read the examples, try the little questions before opening the answers, and keep going even if you forget a word. The same words and sound-meaning connections will come back.

You do **not** need to memorize everything in each lesson before continuing.

## Pronunciation

### Consonants

<audio controls style="width:100%">
  <source src="../../course_warmups/audio/consonants.mp3" type="audio/wav">
</audio>

**B C D F G H J L M N P R S T V W Y**

Most can be pronounced as you would expect in English or IPA. A few are worth pointing out:

- **C** as in *cake* — /k/
- **H** as in *house* — /h/; /ʁ/ or /x/ are also fine
- **J** as in *jello* — /dʒ/
- **R** like the *tt* in American *butter* — /ɾ/; /r/ is also fine
- **Y** as in *yes* — /j/

### Vowels

<audio controls style="width:100%">
  <source src="../../course_warmups/audio/vowels.mp3" type="audio/wav">
</audio>

**A E I O U**

- **A** as in *father* — /ɑ/
- **E** as in *cellar* — /ɛ/
- **I** as in *creek* — /i/
- **O** as in *door* — /ɔ/
- **U** as in *flu* — /u/

Closed **e** and **o**, as in Spanish, work too.

### Consonant endings

Some syllables end in a consonant. If that is uncomfortable, add a short unstressed **i** after it:

**morta** → **mor(i)ta**

Both are fine.

## Stress

The preferred stress is on the **penultimate syllable**:

- i**LI**ru
- **MOU**je

With a suffix, the word keeps its original stress and the suffix gets an additional emphasis:

- **MOU**je + **UM**
- **MOU**je**UM**

### Two vowels together

Adjacent vowels are preferably pronounced as one syllable, as in o**RA**via, but two syllables are also acceptable:

- **LI**ria — preferred
- li**RI**a — also acceptable

**iliru · mouje · moujeum · liria**

## One thing to notice

Vocabulary usually takes longer than grammar because words need to be seen multiple times. Oravia gives you recurring sound-meaning connections to help them stick.

Look at these three words:

| Oravia | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **mihie** | horse |
| **mibeh** | goat |
| **mimu** | cow |

Anything jump out?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Reveal</summary>

They all begin with **MI**.

In Oravia, **MI** is connected with **larger animals**. So these words are not three completely unrelated strings of sounds.

You will start seeing how this works in Lesson 1.

</details>

That's enough vocabulary structure for now. There is more going on inside Oravia words, but it is more fun to explore the system while you are actually learning it.
