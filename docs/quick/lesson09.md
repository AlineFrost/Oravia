# Lesson 9: Verb Stacking, Numbers 6–10, and NE Gradation

## Grammar

### Verb stacking

When one verb leads into another, repeat **i** before the next verb:

`i [first verb] i [second verb]`

For several very common verbs, the first verb normally appears as its short form. This is simply the root of the verb, without the initial word cluster:

| Full word | Short form in a stack | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **anidai** | **dai** | want |
| **anifou** | **fou** | need |
| **ando** | **do** | can |

`i dai i boemo`

</details>

`i fou i anefene`

</details>

`i do i anvu`

*I can go.*

</details>

### Numbers 6–10

**teva** — 6  
**peri** — 7  
**auta** — 8  
**tenda** — 9  
**das** — 10  

Did you know *oito*, which sounds a bit like *auta*, is *eight* in Portuguese? And *dez*, which sounds a bit like *das*, is ten? 

Larger numbers put the larger unit first:

`das tam` = 11  
`das alui` = 15  
`tor das` = 20  
`par das bale` = 34

### NE gradation: rate it from 0 to 10

**NE** turns a number into a degree scale, always with 10 as reference. Put **ne + number** before something that can naturally vary by degree. For example, let's use ne gradation with the adjective *difficult*:

**nealui yudur** — 5/10 difficult → moderately difficult  
**neauta yudur** — 8/10 difficult → very difficult  
**nedas yudur** — 10/10 difficult → extremely difficult  

The scale can include zero as **sunya**:

**nesunya** — 0/10, not at all  

And it can go beyond ten when you want an “over the scale” reading:

**nedastam yudur** — 11/10 difficult → too difficult / excessively difficult

The same scale gives you *a little, halfway, very, completely*, and every point in between.

Notice that this can be used for frequency, likelihood, and many others. This system gives precision while avoiding adding dozens of words (such as rather, often and certain) to the core vocabulary.

!!! question "Try it"
    **yaltan** = big, large. If **auta = 8**, what does **neauta yaltan** suggest?

    <details>
    <summary>Reveal</summary>

    **8/10 big**, roughly **very big**.

    </details>

## Vocabulary

### Building blocks

**WIL** — human-made geography: settlements and built connections. *Mnemonic: **village / villa**.*  
**VIS** — organs. *Mnemonic: **viscera**.*  
**WOL** — wide. *Mnemonic: **wall-to-wall**.*  
**NE** — quantity and degree. *Mnemonic: **number**.*  

### Words

| Oravia | Meaning | Breakdown |
|---|---|---|
| **ando** | can, be able | **AN** action + **DO** regulations (permission/possibility) |
| **wiltan** | city | **WIL** human-made geography + **TAN** big |
| **vismo** | stomach | **VIS** organs + **MO** eating |
| **yalwol** | wide | **YAL** size/dimension + **WOL** wide |

Look at **wiltan** and **visomo**:

**wiltan** = built geography + big → **city**  
**visomo** = organ + eating → **stomach**
