# 3.2 — The Cat Is Too Quiet

[← All graded readers](reader.md){ .graded-reader-back }

![Story illustration](../../images/graded-readers/3.2.jpg){ width="480" style="display:block; margin:1.25rem auto 2rem auto;" }

<div class="oravia-reading graded-reader-text">
<p>En limel, wamu i dami en bontame molcui, mai cali falen i anocari de boelori, hay i aparih de molcui.</p>
<p>Cali falen i anivari, a miau i dami en bontame su i anvuum; dantam i vardei e tam jetai.</p>
<p>Falen i iliro ca a miau i nomie no yunitam, eta i widuo su i vardei e ilwol ca miau i vardei.</p>
<p>Notor i siyal e wamu en bontame, widuo de vanpai de miau.</p>
<p>Yasoi, i anefe e wamu en molcui su i nordau e molcui de miau.</p>
<p>Su falen i ilaluan: “Nim i ilian ce run i anidai! Run i anitaum.”</p>
<p>Mai a miau i nomie no varsusum. Dantam i anlaro vardei e wamu.</p>
</div>

<details>
<summary>Show English translation</summary>

<p>At night, a fish is in its bowl on the table, but when the child leaves the room, it jumps out from the bowl.</p>
<p>When the child returns, the cat is on the table and is not moving; it is staring at one direction.</p>
<p>The child thinks the cat is acting strangely, so they come closer and look at whatever the cat is watching.</p>
<p>Then they find the fish on the table, close to the cat’s paw.</p>
<p>Quickly, they put the fish in the bowl and move the bowl away from the cat.</p>
<p>And the child says, “I know what you want! You won't take it.”</p>
<p>But the cat doesn't seem to listen. It just keeps looking at the fish.</p>

</details>
